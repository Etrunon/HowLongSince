## Store listing

**App name:** How Long Since?

### English (en-US)

**Short description** (max 80):
Track how long since you last did anything. Tap once and it counts the days.

**Full description:**
How long since you last watered the plants? Backed up your laptop? Changed the toothbrush? Followed up with that client? Called grandma? Checked the server logs?

How Long Since? keeps one card for each thing you want to keep track of. Tap it when you do it, and it counts the days for you. The most overdue come first.

• Reminders: get a notification when something has gone too long, or let the app use your own average.
• NFC tags: stick a tag on the plant pot or the filter and tap it with your phone to log it, even with the app closed.
• History and stats: every time is logged, with notes, the gaps between them and your average interval.
• Widget and launcher shortcuts.
• Groups: share counters with family or flatmates, on phones and in any browser, with a link or a QR code.

Private by design: counters that aren't in a shared group never leave your phone. Shared groups are end-to-end encrypted, so the server stores data it can't read. No ads, no account, no analytics, no tracking.

Free and open source (GPL-3.0). You can host your own server.

## Data safety

Only sync (optional, user-started) sends anything. Labels, notes, tags, names and who-did-what are end-to-end encrypted. What the server can read, in the clear, is metadata (see the privacy page).

- **Does your app collect or share user data?** Yes.
- **Is all data encrypted in transit?** Yes (HTTPS).
- **Can users request data deletion?** Yes: deleting a group deletes it from the server.
- **Data types collected**, each: collected (not shared), optional, purpose **App functionality**, not processed ephemerally:
  - **App activity → Other actions:** reset times and counter settings (reminder interval, archived, deleted).
  - **Device or other IDs:** random per-device ids and access tokens for group members.
- **Not collected:** name, email, location, contacts, personal info. Labels and notes are end-to-end encrypted and unreadable by the server, so they're exempt from disclosure.
- **IP addresses** the server sees only to answer requests. If the server or its proxy keeps them in logs, the conservative reading is that "Device or other IDs" above covers them.
